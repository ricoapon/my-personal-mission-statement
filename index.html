Personal mission statement

Rico Apon

These are the principles I want to live by. The idea of writing a personal mission statement came from Stephen Covey’s book The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People.

❯ Be imperfect

We are only human. On this page, I write my statements in confident claims. But of course, I will not be able to follow this document to the letter on every occasion. And that is ok. I will do my best, and make amends for my mistakes. And most importantly: treat others the same way. I don't need to be perfect, and neither do you.

❯ Connect

Making a connection with another human being is one of the most amazing feelings for me. To be able to connect, I listen. I am truly interested in the other person. I see the person for who they are, not what they do.

❯ Communicate

Communication is key. This is an often-repeated saying, and I fully believe in this. I have experienced often enough that when communication stops, relationships break. You guess what the other person is thinking and make it worse by not discussing what you are actually feeling. Whatever happens, no matter how hard: communicate. Talk to each other. Everything will turn out just fine. Which can also mean deciding to not be in each other's life anymore.

❯ Learn and grow

I fully believe in the growth mindset: as long as you spend time and energy, you can learn anything. Of course, we all have our limits and I will not be able to do "anything". But believing that I can, pushes me to try. There is always more to learn and I really enjoy learning! I am always open to feedback. I make mistakes, and that is ok. Learn from these mistakes.

❯ Challenge you

I have made a lot of growth steps myself. When I see similar opportunities for you, I challenge you to grow as well. This might not always feel comfortable for you. What I say might feel painful. To me, it shows that I truly care. I want you to become the best version of yourself. Whatever I can do to help, I will. Though sometimes I do not realize you don't want my help. Just tell me if that is the case and I will stop.

❯ Act with integrity

I say what I do and I do what I say. I am honest and I keep to my agreements. This makes me reliable. And it can also make me come across as harsh. I value telling you my honest opinion, which to me is being kind. Others don't always feel this way.

❯ Help others fulfill their dream

I want to share my knowledge and skills with others, so they can reach their dream. I want to see others fly. But the responsibility for achieving this will always be theirs.

❯ Invest time in relations

You can see that I value connections with others by how much time I spend on it. I will choose to spend time with friends over other activities. If a friend is in need, I will make time. I will make sure my own boundaries and needs will be met, which is often the case. Therefore, I have a lot of space in my life that I love to dedicate to people I care about.

❯ Think win/win

If the other person doesn't like the situation, then I don't like it either. I want to find a way we are both happy with. And I will go as far as pushing the other person to stand up for themselves to me if I feel they are accommodating me too much at their own cost.

❯ Be humble

I used to be arrogant. I considered myself to be better than others. I learned that this quote is a far better way to live: Impress yourself, not others. Sometimes it might feel a bit awkward to me to show off something that I am excited about, because I am trying to be humble but at the same time over-the-top enthusiastic. My enthusiasm will always win. And I want it that way.

Don’t explain your philosophy. Embody it. — After Epictetus
